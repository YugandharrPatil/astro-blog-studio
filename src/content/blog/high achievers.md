high achievers in any domain like business, sports, programming don't get there by sheer hardwork or grit. or by "pushing themselves" hard even when there was no hope and just grinding blindly. maybe leetcode grinders do, but they're not exceptional people. exceptional people are those who create value, not those who grind shitty to get accepted in a shitty competition where the herd is heading.

high achiever often say in interviews that they worked their asses off, worked super hard, for 14 hours a day, n shit. but that is rarely the "actual" reason they're successful. rather, they're successful because of something much much deeper.

their success has to do something with the fact that they stuck to their craft because they had an internal obsession, longing, something inside them, that couldn't be named, that had then pushing at their craft repeatedly. it can be different for different people. some have the motivation by looking at the goal they wanna achieve (the end state of success), some just love the process and the dopamine they get from having grinded and achieved the output, etc.
but the point is that it is rarely grit and hard work.

you can name 100s who work just as hard, maybe even harder, got the same opportunity, but their internal self or their DNA just didn't allow them to reach that level. this is the state of millions but we never know it because the media only sells success stories and you have to know, survivorship bias exists.

# discipline

here's the real reason why someone's disciplined:
it is not that they're pushing themselves even in situations of low motivation, just pure grit, pushing through hard times, showing up even when they're feeling lazy n shit.
it is because, when they do that activity, something inside their brain likes it, such that they just want to do it again n again. there is that internal feeling, it is very subtle and very hard to describe, where doing that thing just creates an intricate feedback loop where the person just wants to do the activity again. it is not fun exactly, but a very subtle feeling.

it is the same story with obsession. it isn't the very thing the're obsessed about that they like. rather, there is this subtle internal feeling that pursuing that activity brings to a person. be in passion in tennis, football, coding (different for AI, cybersec, game dev, etc). therefore, it tells you a lot about a person's character. each of these domains create very different internal stimulus and therefore, different domains interest different people. therefore, it is not possible to manufacture a passion or obsession per say. it is a lot linked to your brain and your DNA and what really resonates with you to get that subtle internal feeling.

this eludes to the point that the thing is not about the thing.
